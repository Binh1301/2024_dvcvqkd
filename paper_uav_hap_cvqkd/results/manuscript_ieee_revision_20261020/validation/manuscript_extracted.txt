Abstract—This provisional internal-review study examines
source selection and UA V positioning for a cloud-affected HAP–
UA V discrete-modulated CV-QKD model. A ﬁnite catalog of
233 exact-source ensembles deﬁnes a common location-dependent
numerical rate map. An ofﬂine stationary benchmark, a current-
slot Greedy policy, and ﬁnite-horizon dynamic programming
(DP) are compared under identical cloud realizations and speed
constraints. Across 100 paired missions, expanding the candidate
set from 10 to 100 raises the mean DP penalized objective by
11.41%. At blockage probability 0.8, mean zero-rate unavailabil-
ity is 24.60%, 16.53%, and 4.07% for Stationary, Greedy, and
DP, respectively. At the representative penalty, DP reduces travel
by 88.37% relative to zero penalty while reducing mean usable
rate by 0.515%. These are conditional manuscript-model outputs:
the recorded two-dimensional mutual information and homodyne
Holevo expression do not specify a consistent Bob measurement.
The results characterize the saved numerical optimization and
do not establish physical quantum security.
Index Terms—Continuous-variable quantum key distribution,
UA V , high-altitude platform, cloud blockage, probabilistic shap-
ing, dynamic programming.

B. Impact of Candidate UA V Positions
Figure 1 compares deterministic nested subsets J =
10, 20, 40, 60, 80, 100 at λref . The mean DP objective increases
from 1.39004 × 10−4 to 1.54863 × 10−4 bit/use, an 11.41%
increase relative to J = 10. P2 increases from 1.36348×10−4
to 1.50243 × 10−4, and Greedy from 1.07800 × 10−4 to
1.48311 × 10−4 bit/use. Adding positions preserves existing
feasible paths, so optimal P2 and DP objectives cannot de-
crease; Greedy has no such general guarantee.
At J = 20 , DP reaches 96.71% of its J = 100 objective.
However, the J = 10 transition graph has eight connected
components and seven isolated positions, whereas the sampled
subsets are connected from J = 20 onward. The initial
improvement combines expanded spatial choices and connec-
tivity changes; it does not identify an economically optimal
grid size or isolate candidate density alone.
C. Impact of Cloud Blockage Probability
Figure 2(a) shows decreasing mean usable SKR as p in-
creases from 0.1 to 0.8 for all three methods. DP decreases
from 1.57663 × 10−4 to 1.43475 × 10−4 bit/use. The proba-
bility masks are nested under common innovations: increasing
p removes usable rewards. Therefore, the optimal P2 and DP
objectives cannot increase; a general availability or Greedy
ordering does not follow.
The DP-minus-P2 mean objective difference grows from
1.11215 × 10−7 to 2.72806 × 10−5 bit/use. Dividing each
difference by its corresponding P2 mean gives gains of 0.071%
and 25.042%. At p = 0 .8, the paired 95% interval for the
absolute difference is [2.46760, 2.99896] × 10−5 bit/use. The
larger relative gain is consequently accompanied by a larger
absolute gain. No interior maximum occurs on the evaluated
probability grid, and no claim is made beyond p = 0.8.
In Fig. 2(b), unavailability at p = 0 .8 is 24.60% for P2,
16.53% for Greedy, and 4.07% for DP . These observed avail-
ability beneﬁts do not mean that P3 maximizes availability.
Greedy’s mean objective is below P2 at p = 0.1–0.4; its paired
differences at p = 0.5 and 0.7 have intervals containing zero.
At p = 0.8, its objective exceeds P2 by 8.54160×10−6 bit/use,
less than DP’s improvement.

E. SKR–Mobility Tradeoff
Figure 4 connects saved policies in the original order of
ten increasing penalties. At λ = 0 , DP achieves mean usable
SKR 1.57305 × 10−4 bit/use and distance 1482.69 m. At λref ,
distance falls to 172.43 m and mean SKR to 1.56495 × 10−4
bit/use. Relative to the respective zero-penalty means on the
same 100 default missions, these changes are an 88.37%
distance reduction and a 0.515% SKR reduction.
At λref , Greedy travels 430.61 m, while DP has both higher
mean SKR and higher objective. DP’s SKR gains relative to
P2 and Greedy are 4.162% and 2.695%; its objective gains
are 3.075% and 4.418%, respectively. The paired DP-minus-
P2 objective interval is [3.56503, 5.73139] × 10−6 bit/use. The
distinction matters because movement is penalized only in U .
At sampled penalties 0.1λhi and above, all saved DP paths
are stationary and coincide with P2 in objective. This does not
identify a continuous stopping threshold. Greedy can stop at
a worse location because it chooses its initial position from
the ﬁrst slot. Optimal DP distance is nonincreasing with λ
for the same feasible set and rewards; the plotted points are
evaluated numerical operating choices, not global physical
Pareto frontiers.
F . Discussion and Limitations
Across the investigated parameter ranges, the largest mean-
SKR span occurs for candidate count in DP , speed in Greedy,
and blockage probability in P2. These are descriptive ranges
over unequal domains, not normalized sensitivity indices.
Among cloud sweeps, the largest relative DP-versus-P2 ob-
jective gain occurs at p = 0 .8, while the largest absolute
difference occurs at ρ = 0 . The supplementary spatial sweep
also distinguishes absolute performance from mobility gain:
increasing ℓ from 50 to 1000 m reduces absolute perfor-
mance but increases the DP-minus-P2 objective difference
from 2.25288 × 10−6 to 8.10831 × 10−6 bit/use. Full-grid
blockage grows from zero to 2.70% of slots. A larger relative
gain therefore does not imply increasing spatial diversity.
P1 provides the common catalog-selected map, P2 captures
ofﬂine ﬁxed-position selection, and DP-minus-P2 measures the
extra value of permitting movement in that setting. Since all
positions select the same source, cloud-avoidance gains cannot
be assigned to adaptive shaping. These three methods do not
isolate matched-energy shaping gain or stationary-selection
gain relative to a ﬁxed deployment. DP-minus-Greedy includes
differences in future-state and initial-position information.
Objective dominance does not imply higher SKR in every
positive-penalty mission.
The study is restricted to a ﬁnite source catalog and UA V
grid, synthetic weather and geometry, and complete cloud
blockage. Tracking interruptions, reacquisition, deployment,
return travel, and calibrated propulsion energy are omitted. The
mixed 2D-MI/homodyne model in Section I-B, the effective-
likelihood approximation, and correlation-domain treatment
prevent a validated physical SKR interpretation. Trusted re-
ceiver noise would require a separate consistent model [ 4].
Asymptotic rate arithmetic and mission conﬁdence intervals
establish neither operational nor ﬁnite-key, composable, or
unconditional security.

IV. C ONCLUSION
Finite-catalog source selection and ﬁnite-grid position-
ing provide a reproducible optimization framework for the
recorded HAP–UA V numerical model. Ofﬂine DP maximizes
the stated SKR–distance objective for known clouds, while
P2 selects a ﬁxed location and Greedy uses current-slot
information. Increasing candidate count improves the opti-
mal objective, strong blockage increases the observed beneﬁt
of mobility, and persistent favorable cloud states reduce its
incremental value over ofﬂine stationary selection. At the
representative penalty, DP gains 3.075% in mean objective
over P2 and 4.418% over Greedy on the default missions;
relative to zero-penalty DP it reduces travel by 88.37% with
a 0.515% decrease in mean usable rate. These conclusions
apply to the ﬁnite catalog, synthetic clouds, and distance sur-
rogate. A measurement-consistent MI/Holevo model, justiﬁed
correlation-domain bound, and operational fading/outage secu-
rity analysis are prerequisites for interpreting these conditional
numerical rates as secure key generation. This manuscript
remains provisional for internal review.
