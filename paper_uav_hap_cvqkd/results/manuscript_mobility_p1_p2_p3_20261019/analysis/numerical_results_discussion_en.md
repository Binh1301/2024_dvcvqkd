# Draft: Numerical Results and Discussion

**Numerical-model scope: UNVALIDATED_SECURITY_MODEL; NOT_READY_FOR_PUBLICATION_SCALE_RUNS.**
This is a manuscript discussion draft from saved simulation artifacts. It does not assign an operational security claim or authorize publication evaluation.

## Evaluation setting and information assumptions

We analyze 100 synthetic cloud missions over 100 candidate locations in a 1 km × 1 km region. Each mission comprises 30 slots of 20 s, with a maximum inter-slot displacement of 300 m. The source catalog contains 197 Maxwell–Boltzmann (MB) and 36 Uniform 256-QAM ensembles with authenticated exact source moments. All policies use identical per-location source assignments, cloud masks, speed limits, and a freely selectable initial location. Deployment and return costs are omitted. Stationary positioning and dynamic programming (DP) are offline benchmarks with the complete cloud horizon, whereas Greedy observes all locations at the current slot only. Thus, equal feasibility and initialization assumptions do not imply equal information or identical realized starting locations.

We distinguish the average usable rate K, total distance D, and the objective U=K−λD/1000. The ten penalties were fixed from λhi=0.003156008892 bits/use after source selection and before trajectory evaluation. Unless otherwise stated, the illustrative operating point is λ=9.468026677e-06=0.003λhi. Rates are in bits per channel use; a channel-use rate and propulsion-energy calibration are unavailable.

## Finite-catalog source optimization

All 100 locations select (ν,VA)=(0.125,8), with clear-sky rates ranging from 0.0001162962774 to 0.0001578004446 bits/use (spatial mean 0.0001372424595; population spatial SD 1.308760671e-05). Link distances span 21.6936322–22.14766483 km and zenith angles 28.85627968–30.92081858 degrees. The runner-up is (0.15,8) throughout the region; its rate deficit is 4.332961206e-07–4.50184782e-07 bits/use, approximately 0.275–0.387% of the selected rate. This establishes ranking stability over the observed channel states, while also showing a relatively flat local catalog competition. It does not establish robustness to unswept optical/noise parameters. Moreover, VA=8 is the MB catalog ceiling; no continuous or global source optimum is claimed.

The best Uniform raw rate remains negative at every location, so its usable rate is zero throughout this region. The MB-minus-Uniform usable difference therefore equals the positive MB rate; a percentage gain over zero is undefined. Family-wise optimization also changes modulation variance, so this difference cannot be attributed solely to shaping at fixed energy. An additional comparison using the saved Uniform VA=8 ensemble gives a mean Uniform raw rate of -0.0004565551269 and a mean raw MB-minus-Uniform difference of 0.0005937975863 bits/use at equal mean energy. Equal peak amplitude or hardware requirements are not implied. Since the same MB ensemble is selected everywhere, the subsequent mobility gain does not require source switching along a trajectory.

## Stationary selection under blockage

The protocol already specifies the geometric reference index44 at (450,450) m. Although a separate fixed-reference strategy was not exported in the original run, its rate can be reconstructed directly from the saved rate matrix. This is a post-analysis baseline at an existing reference point, not a newly selected or preregistered operational baseline. Its mean rate is 8.101245823e-05, compared with 0.0001502428187 bits/use for optimal stationary selection, a ratio-of-means increase of 85.45643712%. The paired absolute increase is 6.923036043e-05, with a Student-t 95% interval [6.242732497e-05, 7.60333959e-05].

The selected stationary point varies across 34 locations. Indices40 and90 are most frequent, each selected in eight missions. Mean cloud-free availability rises from 58.06666667% at the reference to 97.76666667%; 67 missions permit 30 clear slots at the chosen point. The highest clear-sky-rate location is selected in only eight missions, while 72 selections attain maximal mission cloud-free fraction. A symmetric algebraic decomposition attributes 84.03451993% of the mean reference-to-stationary gain to the availability term. This decomposition is descriptive, not causal, but indicates that cloud avoidance dominates the additional clear-sky-rate improvement in this scenario. The gain assumes offline stationary selection with the complete mission realization.

## Mobility gain and statistical variability

At the illustrative penalty, DP attains mean rate 0.0001564953527 and mean distance 172.4329898 m, compared with 0.0001523879118 and 430.6101833 m for Greedy. DP increases mean rate by 4.161619209% over Stationary and 2.695384988% over Greedy. Mean objective improvements are 4.619933854e-06 (3.074978155%) and 6.551869453e-06 (4.417659256%), respectively. The reported percentages use differences of sample means divided by the positive baseline mean, rather than means of per-mission ratios.

We resample whole paired missions, preserving all within-mission spatial and temporal dependence and the common scenarios across methods and penalties. A 20,000-resample paired percentile bootstrap with seed20261019 yields 95% intervals [3.57732301e-06, 5.736504879e-06] for DP-minus-Stationary objective and [4.999201071e-06, 8.46048159e-06] for DP-minus-Greedy. Both are positive at the illustrative operating point. Nevertheless, DP strictly improves objective in only 61 and89 missions, respectively, with the remainder tied within 1e−12 bits/use. Unpenalized DP rate is lower than Greedy in25 missions, consistent with optimizing the penalized objective. These are pointwise intervals for mean numerical performance, without simultaneous multiplicity correction; they do not establish physical security or held-out generalization.

## Penalty-dependent movement and the role of lookahead

At λ=0, DP yields mean rate 0.0001573051191 with mean travel 1482.687834 m. Increasing λ reduces DP travel in every evaluated mission, and all DP solutions become stationary at the tested penalty 0.0003156008892=0.1λhi. This monotonicity follows from the common feasible trajectory set and the linear distance penalty. The sampled penalties bracket the transition; they do not identify its exact continuous value. In contrast, Greedy has31 adjacent-penalty transitions with increased distance across25 missions. The initial divergence can favor a shorter local move at the larger penalty while changing subsequent routing. Because the same missions are reused, this is a deterministic finite-grid policy effect, not independent cloud resampling. At high penalties Greedy stops at its first-slot choice and can remain inferior to the horizon-optimal stationary solution despite zero travel.

Mission84, selected post hoc as the largest DP-minus-Stationary objective gain at the fixed illustrative penalty, shows why future information can help. DP avoids all blocked slots with five relocations and847.214m travel, compared with seven and1088.635m for Greedy. At slot2, DP moves from81 to80 while both locations are currently clear: its immediate objective increment relative to staying is negative, but remaining at81 would encounter21 blocked slots in the residual horizon. Across100 missions, 52 of112 DP moves have a negative immediate increment, in38 missions; their saved continuations have nonnegative residual gain over the feasible counterfactual of remaining at the previous location. These checks use stored trajectories and rate arrays only. They demonstrate anticipatory decisions but do not isolate the causal contribution of lookahead from oracle initialization and other decision differences.

Mission9 gives identical Greedy and DP paths, rates, objectives, and200m travel; a single reactive relocation suffices. Mission0 retains the original preselected illustration: DP and Stationary coincide at index80 with no travel, while Greedy travels100m for a small rate increment that does not offset the penalty. The three post hoc examples are explanatory and do not replace the100-mission statistics.

## Conditions for useful mobility and model limitations

Mobility is most valuable when no single high-rate location remains clear throughout the horizon but reachable alternatives offer complementary clear slots. At the illustrative penalty, all33 missions whose optimal stationary location experiences blockage obtain a positive DP objective gain; their mean increase is9.851524932e−6 bits/use, compared with2.043180039e−6 in the67 missions with an entirely clear stationary point. Seven missions have zero headroom between the per-slot unconstrained maximum and the best stationary mean and exhibit no mobility gain. These groups are exploratory, conditioned on the observed cloud matrices.

The results support finite-source-catalog and finite-grid numerical comparisons under synthetic complete blockage. They omit transit and reacquisition interruptions, cloud-estimation/prediction errors, deployment/return costs, calibrated propulsion energy, and an operational channel-use rate. The mixed two-dimensional MI/homodyne-Holevo functional remains unvalidated as a physical homodyne security rate. Passing numerical checks or obtaining narrow mission-level intervals does not resolve attack-model, finite-size, parameter-estimation, or composable-security requirements. UNVALIDATED_SECURITY_MODEL and NOT_READY_FOR_PUBLICATION_SCALE_RUNS remain unchanged.
