Shulei Gong , Hong Shen , Member, IEEE, Kanglian Zhao , Member, IEEE,
Ruhai Wang , Senior Member, IEEE, Xinggan Zhang, Tomaso de Cola , Member, IEEE,
and Juan A. Fraire , Member, IEEE
Abstract—This letter considers availability maximized ground
station (GS) selection for free-space optical (FSO) satellite
networks, which is a challenging problem in the area of optical
satellite communications. As a main contribution of this letter,
we propose to address this problem from a new perspective of
mathematical optimization. In particular, we advocate a gradi-
ent projection based algorithm after performing smoothing and
relaxation on the original problem. Simulation results and com-
plexity analysis show that, when compared to the conventional GS
selection methods, the proposed scheme achieves a better tradeoff
between network availability and computational complexity.
Index Terms —Network availability, free-space optical (FSO)
communications, satellite communications, ground station (GS)
selection.
I. I NTRODUCTION

REE-SPACE optical (FSO) communications have
attracted a lot of attention in both academia and industry.
As an appealing advantage, FSO communications can achieve
much higher data rates than conventional radio frequency
(RF) communications since the optical band provides much
broader bandwidth. On the other hand, the required data rates
in modern satellite communications, e.g., in feeder links of
geostationary Earth orbit (GEO) satellites, have been increas-
ing tremendously. Among practical wireless solutions, FSO
communications have turned out to be promising for next-
generation high-speed satellite networks [1]. For instance, the
relay link from low Earth orbit (LEO) to GEO in the European
Data Relay System (EDRS) has been implemented via optical

Despite the high capacity available in the optical band,
the main drawback of FSO satellite communications, espe-
cially on space-to-ground links, lies in the sensitivity to cloud
blockage. Due to the presence of clouds, the outage of opti-
cal links can seriously deteriorate the system performance. A
widely adopted strategy to resolve this problem is site diver-
sity [11]–[17], i.e., increasing the number of optical links
with multiple ground stations (GSs) (see Fig. 1). In fact,
owing to the reliability enhancement provided by the site
diversity, network availability, which refers to the probabil-
ity that at least one GS is connected to the satellite, can
be greatly enhanced. Speciﬁcally, the authors of [11] calcu-
lated both uncorrelated and correlated availabilities for optical
space-to-ground networks. Moreover, an efﬁcient GS selection
algorithm was developed by exploiting the single-site avail-
ability and the correlation between sites. In [12], a two-step
method was proposed to jointly optimize the GS positioning
and the satellite operator backbone. In order to avoid com-
putationally inefﬁcient discrete-time simulations, the authors
of [13] developed several approximation methods to estimate
the availability of the optical space-to-ground network. In [14],
the authors adopted the maximization of the amount of data
that can be downloaded from a LEO satellite as the GS
selection criterion. The corresponding problem is formulated
by a mixed integer program and solved with a hierarchical
method. The optimal location of GSs was determined for
LEO satellite networks in [15] where the network availabil-
ity, the latency between successive contacts, and the network
cost were considered as performance metrics. Based on the
monthly statistics of cloud coverage, the authors of a recent
work [16] minimized the number of GSs while satisfying the
monthly network availability requirements. In [17], the authors
studied the problem of minimizing the number of GSs under
the network availability constraints for medium Earth orbit
(MEO) satellite communication networks.
In this letter, we develop an efﬁcient GS selection algorithm
to maximize the availability of GEO satellite networks. The
main contributions of this letter are listed as follows:
• Different from the aforementioned related works, we for-
mulate the GS selection as a mathematical optimization
problem with the objective of maximizing the network
availability, and then solve the resultant problem using
powerful optimization techniques. To the best of our
knowledge, this is a novel perspective of studying the
GS selection for FSO satellite communications.
• The formulated problem is a difﬁcult non-smooth integer
program which has not been studied in related literature.

III. P ERFORMANCE AND COMPLEXITY ANALYSIS
In this section, we validate the effectiveness of the proposed
GS selection algorithm by comparing with the optimal exhaus-
tive search method and two other baseline algorithms in [11],
i.e., the single-site availability (SSA) based method and the
site correlation & single-site availability (SC & SSA) based
method, in terms of both performance and computational com-
plexity. Note that the proposed method consists of two parts,
i.e., Algorithm 1 and Algorithm 2. Moreover, we adopt the
low-complexity SSA based method to generate the initial point
of Algorithm 1.
We consider a scenario with 100 GSs in total. The number
of data points for each GS equals 50000. In order to con-
sider the correlation between the GSs, we generate the binary
cloud data matrix based on the correlated Bernoulli distri-
bution. Speciﬁcally, following [13, Sec. II-B], we ﬁrst draw
samples from an N-dimensional normal distribution N (µ,Γ),
where the elements of µ follow the uniform distribution over
[−1, 1] and the ( m, n)-th entry of Γ is 9 × 0.9|m−n|. Then, we
obtain the correlated Bernoulli distributed samples by truncat-
ing the normally distributed samples. For each time sample
j, we repeat the above procedure and ﬁnally obtain the cloud
data matrix. For the proposed Algorithm 1, the step sizes are
chosen according to the widely used Armijo rule [20] and the
convergence accuracy is set to 10−3.

method is close to that of the SC & SSA based method and
the optimal exhaustive search. In fact, the difference between
the proposed method and the exhaustive search is zero when
K = 2 and K = 4. The performance of the exhaustive search
for K > 4 is not obtained because the computational complex-

method and the near-optimal SC & SSA based method is
minor (less than 2 × 10−4 when K ≥ 8), we can infer that
the proposed method performs close to the exhaustive search

IV . CONCLUSION
Optical satellite communications achieve high data rate
while suffering from link outages caused by the presence of
clouds. In this letter, we considered the problem of maximiz-
ing the network availability for optical GEO satellite networks
assuming that cloud data and the number of required active
GSs are given. We proposed an efﬁcient GS selection algo-
rithm based on the gradient projection method to address
this problem. Compared to the conventional GS selection
methods, the proposed method achieves a better performance-
complexity tradeoff. Future research efforts will be made to
evaluate the proposed algorithm with the real cloud database.
