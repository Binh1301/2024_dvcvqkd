Abstract—This provisional internal-review study examines
source selection and UA V positioning for a cloud-affected HAP–
UA V discrete-modulated CV-QKD model. A ﬁnite catalog of
233 exact-source ensembles deﬁnes a common location-dependent
numerical rate map. An ofﬂine stationary benchmark, a current-
slot Greedy policy, and ﬁnite-horizon dynamic programming
(DP) are compared under identical cloud realizations and speed
constraints. Across 100 paired missions, expanding the candidate
set from 10 to 100 raises the mean DP penalized objective by
11.41%. At blockage probability 0.8, mean zero-rate unavailabil-
ity is 24.60%, 16.53%, and 4.07% for Stationary, Greedy, and
DP, respectively. At the representative penalty, DP reduces travel
by 88.37% relative to zero penalty while reducing mean usable
rate by 0.515%. These are conditional manuscript-model outputs:
the recorded two-dimensional mutual information and homodyne
Holevo expression do not specify a consistent Bob measurement.
The results characterize the saved numerical optimization and
do not establish physical quantum security.
Index Terms—Continuous-variable quantum key distribution,
UA V , high-altitude platform, cloud blockage, probabilistic shap-
ing, dynamic programming.

IV. N UMERICAL RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Table I gives the synthetic baseline; cell centers are
50, 150, . . . ,950 m per axis. Gaussian-copula clouds use spa-
tial covariance exp(−di,j/ℓ) and latent AR(1) coefﬁcient ρ.
Comparisons share 100 mission IDs (0–99); uncertainty uses
saved whole-mission bootstrap intervals. Availability means
S > 0, without an engineering threshold. The presentation
follows [ 3].
Fig. 1 compares the positioning schemes as blockage in-
creases. Observed usable rates decrease, but movement can
avoid blocked locations. At p = 0 .8, mean zero-rate unavail-
ability is 24.60%, 16.53%, and 4.07% for Stationary, Greedy,
and DP , respectively. This availability beneﬁt is observed; P3
optimizes U , not positive-rate slot count.
Fig. 2 shows persistent favorable locations making ofﬂine
stationary selection competitive. As ρ increases from 0 to 0.98,
mean DP distance falls from 1038.25 to 31.84 m, while its
mean objective gain over P2 falls from 23.712% to 0.155%.
This sampled trend is not a correlation monotonicity theorem
or an online result. Enlarging nested position sets, however,
cannot lower optimal P2/DP U : increasing J from 10 to 100
improves mean DP U by 11.409%.
At the default conﬁguration and λref , DP improves mean us-
able rate by 4.16% over P2 and 2.70% over Greedy, and mean
objective by 3.07% and 4.42%, respectively. Relative to zero-
penalty DP , the representative penalty reduces mean travel by
88.370% with only a 0.515% decrease in mean usable rate. All
locations select the same MB source (ν, VA) = (0 .125, 8) at
the catalog boundary; these gains do not isolate shaping gain.
Rates retain the security limitations of Section I-B; ﬁnite-key,
tracking, and nonideal-detector effects [ 4] are excluded.
