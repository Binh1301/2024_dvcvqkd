Abstract—This provisional internal-review study examines
source selection and UA V positioning for a cloud-affected HAP–
UA V discrete-modulated CV-QKD model. A ﬁnite catalog of
233 exact-source ensembles deﬁnes a common location-dependent
numerical rate map. An ofﬂine stationary benchmark, a current-
slot Greedy policy, and ﬁnite-horizon dynamic programming
(DP) are compared under identical cloud realizations and speed
constraints. Across 100 paired missions, expanding the candidate
set from 10 to 100 raises the mean DP penalized objective by
11.41%. At blockage probability 0.8, mean zero-rate unavailabil-
ity is 24.60%, 16.53%, and 4.07% for Stationary, Greedy, and
DP, respectively. At the representative penalty, DP reduces travel
by 88.37% relative to zero penalty while reducing mean usable
rate by 0.515%. These are conditional manuscript-model outputs:
the recorded two-dimensional mutual information and homodyne
Holevo expression do not specify a consistent Bob measurement.
The results characterize the saved numerical optimization and
do not establish physical quantum security.
Index Terms—Continuous-variable quantum key distribution,
UA V , high-altitude platform, cloud blockage, probabilistic shap-
ing, dynamic programming.

IV. N UMERICAL RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
The synthetic HAP/UA V altitudes are 20/1 km; the 1 km2
grid has 100 centers spaced 100 m apart. Missions have 30×20
s slots, vmax = 15 m/s, and Dref = 1000 m. The synthetic
deployment places the HAP horizontally at (11419.6551, 450)
m and uses UA V coordinates 50, 150, . . . ,950 m along each
axis. Saved missions use seed 20261015 and realization IDs 0–
99. The retained optical settings are 1550 nm, full divergence
233 µrad, receiver diameter 0.08 m, βrec = 0.95, ξch = 0.0186
SNU, and ideal detection. Saved Gaussian-copula complete-
blockage clouds default to (p, ℓ, ρ) = (0 .4, 250 m, 0.9), with
latent spatial covariance Σij = exp( −di,j/ℓ) and a sta-
tionary AR(1) coefﬁcient ρ. Latent parameters differ from
Bernoulli-cloud correlations. Each latent state is thresholded at
Φ−1(p), where Φ is the standard-normal distribution function;
the marginal blockage probability is prescribed, rather than
measured meteorology. We compare 100 paired missions at
ﬁxed λref = 9 .468026677248516 × 10−6 bit/use. Means
and pointwise conﬁdence intervals use independent whole
missions, not cells or slots; methods share each realization
ID and conﬁgurations use common innovations. No new
physical-layer evaluation is required. These intervals describe
mission variability, rather than ﬁnite-key parameter-estimation
uncertainty or a quantum-security bound. Availability means
S > 0, without an engineering threshold. In Fig. 1(a),
expanding deterministic nested position sets from J = 10
to 100 raises mean DP U by 11.409% relative to J = 10 .
The improvement combines more spatial choices and greater
connectivity; optimal P2/DP objectives cannot decrease as the
feasible set expands. The comparative presentation follows [ 3].
At p = 0 .8, mean unavailability is 24.60%, 16.53%, and
4.07% for P2, Greedy, and DP , respectively [Fig. 1(b)]. Cloud-
aware movement improves this observed metric, although
P3 optimizes U . Increasing p under nested masks removes
rewards, so optimal P2/DP objectives cannot increase; no
general availability ordering follows. As latent ρ increases
from 0 to 0.98, DP distance decreases from 1038.25 to 31.84
m [Fig. 1(c)], while its mean objective gain over P2 decreases
from 23.712% to 0.155%, relative to the corresponding P2
mean. Persistent favorable locations make ofﬂine stationary
selection competitive. At the high-correlation endpoint, the
latent persistence time exceeds the mission horizon, allowing
P2 to exploit locations favorable throughout the mission. This
is not a universal monotonic law or a demonstration with
forecast errors.
On the same default missions, changing DP from zero
penalty to λref reduces mean distance by 88.370% and mean
usable SKR by 0.515%, relative to their respective zero-
penalty means. Thus, most movement can be avoided with a

V. C ONCLUSION
Finite-catalog source selection, ofﬂine stationary position-
ing, and DP trajectory optimization jointly characterize cloud-
aware HAP–UA V performance. Under the evaluated conﬁg-
urations, DP improves the mobility-penalized objective over
Stationary and Greedy, with observed availability beneﬁts.
A distance penalty substantially reduces movement while
preserving most usable rate. Conclusions remain limited to
a ﬁnite grid, synthetic clouds, ofﬂine future information,
and an uncalibrated mobility-cost surrogate. The inconsistent
MI/Holevo measurement pairing requires correction before
interpreting numerical rates as secure keys.
