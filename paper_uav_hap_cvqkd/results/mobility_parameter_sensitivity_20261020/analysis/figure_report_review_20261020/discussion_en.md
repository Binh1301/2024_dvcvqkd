These paragraphs describe the saved synthetic finite-catalog/grid experiment. They do not establish operational or composable quantum security. The symbol K denotes the manuscript-model usable-SKR metric.

At the representative penalty of 9.468026677248516e-6 bit/use, the default DP solution achieves mean K=0.00015649535 and U=0.00015486275 bit/use with a mean travel distance of 172.433 m. Its mean objective exceeds the stationary and Greedy references by 3.075% and 4.418%, respectively. The paired 95% confidence intervals for these relative gains are [2.350, 3.854]% and [3.325, 5.788]%. DP also travels 59.96% less than Greedy. P2 and DP use offline cloud states, whereas Greedy observes only the current slot; their difference therefore includes an information advantage.

Figures A1 and A2 show the benefit of enlarging the nested candidate set. Increasing J from 10 to 100 raises the DP mean usable SKR by 11.51% and its objective by 11.41%. At the representative penalty, DP zero-SKR unavailability decreases from 7.4% to 0.1%, compared with 9.47% to 2.23% for P2 and 29.57% to 1.27% for Greedy. The J=10 transition graph contains eight connected components, while the tested subsets are connected from J=20 onward. Accordingly, the early improvement reflects both expanded spatial choices and connectivity changes. The J=20 objective reaches 96.71% of the J=100 value; this does not identify an economically optimal candidate count.

Figure B indicates a small incremental benefit beyond the default speed. Raising the limit from 15 to 25 m/s improves the DP objective by only 0.03175%. Relaxing the speed constraint cannot reduce the optimal objective, but it need not increase travel distance: mean DP distance decreases from 177.336 to 176.656 m between 20 and 25 m/s. At zero speed, DP coincides with the offline stationary optimum. Its remaining advantage over Greedy is due to initial-position selection, rather than motion.

Figure C separates worsening absolute performance from increasing adaptation benefit. At blockage probability 0.8, DP retains 95.933% nonzero-SKR availability, and its objective is 25.04% above P2 and 15.95% above Greedy. The absolute DP-minus-P2 objective difference is 2.7280557e-05 bit/use, with a paired 95% interval of [2.4676008e-05, 2.9989589e-05]. The largest observed relative benefit occurs at the upper tested boundary; no interior maximum or extrapolation beyond p=0.8 is inferred.

The supplementary horizon and spatial-correlation results qualify these trends. Increasing Nt from 5 to 30 raises relative DP-versus-P2 objective gain from 0.092% to 3.075%, although absolute average performance declines slightly. Because the objective combines average reward with total travel distance, this sweep does not isolate lookahead from normalization. Increasing latent spatial length from 50 to 1000 m lowers absolute performance but raises the relative DP-versus-P2 objective gain from 1.46% to 5.96%. These observations do not imply a universal monotonic law for horizon or spatial correlation.

The penalty comparison further illustrates the mobility trade-off: at the default configuration, changing from zero to representative penalty reduces DP travel by 88.37% while reducing mean usable SKR by 0.515%. These are changes between saved means, without a newly assigned cross-penalty confidence interval. Objective dominance must not be restated as mission-wise SKR dominance; the latter is reversed in 486 of 2600 case-mission pairs at the positive penalty.

All intervals are pointwise percentile intervals from the existing 20,000 paired whole-mission bootstrap resamples over 100 missions, not independent-slot intervals or quantum-security bounds. Availability means positive usable SKR and is separate from any engineering threshold. No new cloud, quantum/source calculation, optimization run, bootstrap draw, or Stage B experiment was performed for this figure/report continuation.
