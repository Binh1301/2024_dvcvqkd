ABSTRACT This paper introduces an air–ground integrated network (AGIN) architecture for 6G networks
to deliver high-speed connectivity to mobile users (MUs) in hard-to-reach areas. Particularly, the AGIN
architecture integrates a free-space optics (FSO)–based high-altitude platform (HAP) for backhaul and
unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) for last-mile radio-frequency (RF) access. Notably, the HAP, equipped
with an optical intelligent reflecting surface (OIRS), provides FSO-based backhaul links to multiple UAV-
mounted base stations (BSs). For this AGIN architecture, we aim to jointly control UAV placement and
OIRS element allocation to maximize the number of MUs served by the UAVs while minimizing UAV
energy consumption under dynamic backhaul and access link conditions. To this end, we propose a hetero-
geneous centralized training and decentralized execution (CTDE)-based multi-agent deep reinforcement
learning (MADRL) framework that jointly optimizes UAV placement and OIRS element allocation by
employing heterogeneous actor networks for UAV placement and OIRS allocation, respectively. Moreover,
we address the saturation problem through modifications to the tanh function and the actor loss, and
resolve the issue of invalid actions by imposing a constraint on the action output in continuous-action deep
reinforcement learning. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed CTDE-based MADRL framework
effectively maximizes the number of served MUs while reducing UAV energy consumption compared with
state-of-the-art approaches. Additionally, the results confirm the effectiveness of the proposed modifications
in mitigating the saturation problem and demonstrate that incorporating OIRS further enhances network
performance by increasing the number of served MUs while reducing UAV energy consumption.
INDEX TERMS Aerial networks, free-space optics (FSO), optical intelligent reflecting surface (OIRS),
multi-agent deep reinforcement learning (MADRL), joint optimization, heterogeneous actors.
I. Introduction
Rapid advancements in non-terrestrial networks (NTNs) to-
ward beyond 5G (B5G)/6G wireless networks have been
witnessed in recent years [1]. Aerial networks, incorporating
high-altitude platforms (HAPs) and unmanned aerial vehicles
(UAVs), have emerged as a key component of NTNs [2]. To
support extremely high-speed connectivity in such networks,
free-space optical (FSO) communication has attracted con-
siderable attention thanks to its vast available bandwidth [3].
To address the strict line-of-sight requirement of FSO links,
an optical intelligent reflecting surface (OIRS) is considered
as an effective solution [4]. OIRS can extend coverage while
enabling low-cost deployment and low power consumption,
which are especially essential for aerial networks [3].
In this context, OIRS can be deployed on HAP to provide
FSO backhaul links to multiple UAVs. These UAV-mounted
base stations (BSs) then deliver radio-frequency (RF) access
to ground mobile users (MUs). Such an architecture forms
a promising air-ground integrated network (AGIN), par-
ticularly for hard-to-reach and infrastructure-limited areas.
Nevertheless, weather conditions, such as cloud coverage
and atmospheric turbulence, can significantly affect the FSO

and MUs creates a dynamic last-mile RF access network,
further complicating end-to-end network optimization. The
dynamic nature of these networks necessitates real-time
optimization algorithms that can quickly adapt to changing

Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) has recently emerged
as an effective solution for addressing optimization prob-
lems in NTNs [5]. It can adapt to dynamic networks in
real time thanks to its fast inference during deployment.
Existing studies [6], [7] have shown that DRL-based network
optimization can achieve competitive performance while
reducing online computational complexity compared to con-
ventional heuristic approaches. DRL is generally divided into
two categories: single-agent DRL (SADRL) and multi-agent
DRL (MADRL) [8]. SADRL is designed for centralized
execution, which requires global observations. In contrast,
MADRL enables decentralized execution, where only local
observations are available. As a result, MADRL is a potential
approach for the considered AGIN involving multiple UAVs.
MADRL is further classified into decentralized training and
execution (DTE)-based MADRL and centralized training
with decentralized execution (CTDE)-based MADRL [8],
[9]. Recent works have demonstrated that CTDE-based
MADRL significantly outperforms DTE-based MADRL in
cooperative tasks [8], [9]. This is because CTDE lever-
ages global information during training to mitigate non-
stationarity and enable coordinated policies. In contrast, DTE
relies solely on local observations, which may lead to selfish
behavior and suboptimal performance.
In this study, we propose a DRL-based optimization
framework for dynamic AGINs. Since the considered AGIN
requires a high degree of cooperation between UAV place-
ment and OIRS allocation, we adopt a CTDE-based MADRL
framework to enable joint decision-making. To accommodate
the different observation and action spaces of UAV place-
ment and OIRS allocation, we further design heterogeneous
actor networks for the two control tasks. Moreover, we
introduce the modifications to the CTDE-based MADRL
framework to mitigate the saturation problem and prevent
invalid actions in continuous DRL.
A. Motivation
1) Heterogeneous CTDE-based MADRL for AGIN networks
While existing studies [10]–[19] have investigated UAV
placement and OIRS element allocation separately, these two
tasks are inherently coupled in AGINs. Optimizing them
independently may therefore lead to suboptimal network
performance, motivating the adoption of a CTDE-based
MADRL framework for joint control. Furthermore, the UAV
and OIRS agents possess different observation and action
spaces, making homogeneous policy networks in conven-
tional CTDE-based MADRL unsuitable. Therefore, hetero-
geneous policy network architectures should be designed to
accommodate the characteristics of different agents. In this
study, we propose a heterogeneous CTDE-based MADRL
framework, in which each agent’s policy network is designed
according to the characteristics of its action space [20],
[21]. This design is particularly important in MADRL-
based network optimization, where agents control different
network entities with heterogeneous observation and action
properties.
2) Saturation problems and invalid actions in continuous DRL
Recently, current continuous DRL models have faced the
saturation problem [22], which refers to the phenomenon
where the continuous action values output by the tanh or
sigmoid activation functions fall into their saturation regions.
This issue may occur in DRL models with continuous action
spaces, motivating this study to propose modifications to
the DRL framework that prevent the output action values
from falling into these saturation regions. Another concern
in continuous DRL models is that the special characteristics
of agents’ actions must be considered to satisfy system
constraints, while the exploration noise in continuous DRL
models may cause the action outputs to violate these con-
straints. Specifically, the action of the OIRS is constrained by
the total number of available reflecting elements. However,
the exploration noise in continuous DRL may violate this
constraint, resulting in invalid actions.
In summary, although conventional CTDE-based MADRL
has demonstrated promising performance in cooperative op-
timization, it is not well suited for controlling heterogeneous
network entities in AGINs. This limitation arises because
different network entities possess distinct observation and
action characteristics, whereas conventional CTDE methods
employ homogeneous actor network architectures. Further-
more, continuous-control MADRL suffers from saturation
and invalid-action issues, which degrade learning stability
and policy quality. Therefore, this study proposes a hetero-
geneous CTDE-based MADRL framework for AGINs, in
which the saturation and invalid-action issues are mitigated
through the proposed modifications.
B. Key Contributions
The primary objective of this paper is to develop a het-
erogeneous CTDE-based end-to-end optimization framework
for OIRS-aided AGIN with integrated FSO backhaul and
RF access. The proposed framework jointly optimizes UAV
placement and OIRS element allocation to maximize the
number of served MUs while minimizing UAV energy con-
sumption. Tables 1 and 2 provide a clear comparison between
our work and state-of-the-art optimization frameworks for
UAV placement and OIRS element allocation, respectively.
It is worth noting that this study substantially extends our
preliminary work in [23], which developed a DTE-based
MADRL framework for UAV placement with equal OIRS
element allocation under simplified channel models.

II. Related Works
A. Optimization Framework in NTNs
Extensive studies have developed optimization frameworks
for NTNs, particularly focusing on optimal UAV placement,
such as [10]–[16]. Nevertheless, most existing works focus
on RF-based last-mile access, while ignoring the backhaul
or assuming it to be sufficient. Notably, Guan et al. pro-
posed a multi-agent proximal policy optimization (PPO)-
based framework to maximize the number of served users
while preserving overall network throughput in multi-UAV
networks [10]. The authors in [11] proposed a conventional
optimization algorithm for UAV 3D placement and resource
allocation in multihop wireless networks, where backhaul
links depend on UAV positions and access links vary with
user mobility. In [12], the authors investigated a DRL ap-
proach for jointly optimizing the 3D placement of multiple
UAV-mounted BSs under mission-critical scenarios. A DRL-
based framework for multi-UAV placement was provided
in [13] under the assumption of static backhaul networks.
The SADRL-aided end-to-end optimization framework for
mixed FSO/RF-based NTNs was provided in [14]. In [15],
the authors developed a large language model (LLM)-driven
iterative prompting framework for multi-UAV placement in a
static integrated access and backhaul network. Most recently,
the authors in [16] exploited a RL framework integrating the

Additionally, prior works predominantly rely on conventional

not been available in the literature. Such a framework
can improve robustness in agent cooperation and reward
convergence through centralized training. Notably, while
existing studies have employed homogeneous MADRL with
identical actor architectures only for UAV placement, we
propose a heterogeneous CTDE-based MADRL framework
with heterogeneous actor networks to jointly optimize UAV
placement and OIRS allocation.
B. OIRS Element Allocation
Another important concern in OIRS-aided AGIN is the allo-
cation of OIRS elements to facilitate backhaul connectivity
for multiple UAVs over time-varying channels. In other
words, an effective element allocation scheme is essential
for network optimization, which requires a dedicated opti-
mization framework.
Recent studies have investigated OIRS element allocation
strategies [17]–[19]. Specifically, Salam et al. proposed a
distance-based mirror assignment (DMA) scheme to enhance
spectral efficiency and user fairness in multi-user underwater
wireless optical systems [17]. In [18], the authors investi-
gated the energy efficiency maximization in an IRS-assisted
visible light communications (VLC)-RF vehicular system,
which was then addressed by a block coordinate descent
(BCD)-based iterative algorithm. Most recently, the authors
in [19] proposed a two-phase rate-optimized spatial resource
allocation (R-SRA) algorithm for OIRS-aided HAP-assisted
multi-UAV networks.
As summarized in Table 2, existing works [17]–[19]
employ conventional optimization methods for OIRS ele-
ment allocation under static network assumptions. However,
in dynamic environments, e.g., with moving clouds and
UAVs in mobility, such methods cannot effectively adapt to
time-varying conditions. This necessitates an efficient OIRS
element allocation scheme, which is not yet available in the
literature.

Next, Fig. 9 further demonstrates the effectiveness of
the proposed CTDE-based MADRL by comparing it with
the conventional DRL approaches in the second group’s
benchmark schemes. As is expected, the proposed framework
outperforms conventional approaches in both reward conver-
gence and stability. After convergence, while the rewards
of DTE-MADRL and SADRL continue to fluctuate, the
proposed CTDE-MADRL remains stable and consistently
achieves a reward of 3 × 105. Here, the moderate and
fluctuating reward of DTE-MADRL indicates insufficient
cooperation among agents. Also, the low reward of SADRL
is due to combining the rewards of UAV and OIRS agents
despite their distinct, task-specific objectives, demonstrating
that SADRL is unsuitable for such integrated networks.
Moreover, the conventional CTDE-MADRL achieves a sig-
nificantly lower reward due to the saturation problem, high-
lighting the importance of the proposed modifications to the
CTDE-MADRL model.
Additionally, the computational complexity of a MADRL
model mainly stems from the parameter complexity of the
actor and critic networks and the number of agents [37].
Since we do not modify the actor or critic architectures
and continue to employ the same traditional neural network
structures as in conventional MADDPG, the computational
complexity of the proposed MADDPG remains largely com-
parable to that of the original MADDPG. For each agent i, let
P µ

the actor and critic networks, respectively. During centralized
training, each agent updates both its actor and critic networks
using a mini-batch of size S. Therefore, with M + 1 agents
(M UAV agents and 1 OIRS agent), the computational com-
plexity per training iteration is O

During decentralized execution, only actors are employed,
the complexity per execution time step is O ((M + 1)P µ).
The above complexity analysis indicates that the proposed
CTDE-based MADRL framework scales linearly with both
the number of agents and the parameter sizes of the actor
and critic networks. This linear scaling is particularly ad-
vantageous for multi-UAV systems, where the number of
cooperative agents may increase as the network expands.
Therefore, the proposed framework remains computationally
tractable even when the number of UAVs increases mod-
erately. Overall, the complexity analysis confirms that the
proposed CTDE-based MADRL framework preserves the
computational efficiency of the original MADDPG while
providing significantly improved optimization performance
and maintaining good scalability as the number of UAVs
and OIRSs increases.
Figure 10 demonstrates the reward convergence of the
proposed CTDE-based MADRL model and other state-of-
the-art CTDE-based MADRL models. As shown, the pro-
posed CTDE-based MADRL frameworks built on MADDPG
and MATD3 achieve the same converged reward, whereas
FACMAC attains a significantly lower converged reward.
This indicates that our modifications remain effective when
applied to MATD3, but do not perform as well when applied
to FACMAC. In terms of training complexity, MATD3 has
higher complexity because it employs two critic networks,
resulting in an additional complexity of O(2P Q). Similarly,
FACMAC has higher training complexity due to the use
of both an individual critic network and a mixing critic
network, resulting in an additional complexity of O(2P mix).
In contrast, the proposed MADDPG-based framework uses
only one critic network per agent, resulting in an addi-
tional complexity of O(P Q). A comparison of the training
complexities of these CTDE-based MADRL models is pro-
vided in Table 5. These results indicate that the proposed
CTDE-based MADRL framework built on MADDPG is the
most suitable candidate among state-of-the-art CTDE-based
MADRL algorithms for continuous action spaces, offering
the best balance between reward convergence and training
complexity.

VI. Concluding Remarks and Future Directions
This paper presented the AGIN architecture, which inte-
grates the OIRS-aided HAP for FSO backhaul with multiple
UAV-mounted BSs for RF last-mile access. Comprehen-
sive channel models for both HAP-based FSO backhaul
and UAV-based RF access links, together with the UAV
energy consumption model, were provided. The objective
was to jointly maximize the number of served MUs and
minimize UAV energy consumption by optimizing UAV
placement and OIRS element allocation. To this end, we
proposed an improved heterogeneous CTDE-based MADRL
framework based on the MADDPG algorithm. Simulation
results demonstrated that the proposed improved CTDE-
based MADRL outperformed the conventional CTDE-based
MADRL, DTE-based MADRL, and SADRL approaches
under the dynamic network environments. Furthermore, we
confirmed the effectiveness of the proposed DRL-based
OIRS element allocation in increasing the number of served
MUs while minimizing UAV energy consumption compared
to the conventional equal-allocation scheme.
Building upon these findings, future work may explore
the application of heterogeneous CTDE-based MADRL to
broader network optimization problems that require coop-
erative decision-making and real-time implementation. In
addition, further efforts can focus on extending and gen-
eralizing the proposed modifications to address the sat-
uration problem and invalid actions in MADRL models.
Moreover, investigating advanced enhancements such as pre-
dictive learning, multi-HAP, large-scale network scenarios,
and practical constraints (e.g., imperfect CSI, inter-UAV
interference, handover overhead, and hardware limitations)
would further improve the robustness and applicability of
AGIN architectures while exploiting the potential of OIRS
to enhance network performance.
